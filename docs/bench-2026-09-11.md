## 2. The science — dedoping potential vs film degradation
## 2. The science — held privately

The absorbance-modulation analysis across the ladder, and what it says about the film,

What matters here is §1: **the timing measured on a dummy resistor held unchanged on
real films**, which was the open question for the code.


Every film run used `CR09_10mA` (10 mA full scale). MEASURED across all four:

```
largest transient anywhere:  625 uA   (film3, dedoping to -0.5 V)
settled currents:            -0.34 to +33.5 uA
```

**Nothing came within a factor of 1.6 of 1 mA.** `CR10_1mA` was the right range for
every run, and `CR09_10mA` cost:

- quantum **30.5 nA** instead of 3.05 nA
- a **+1.6 µA zero offset** (MEASURED on the dummy 2026-09-11, see §5), which is
  **10–100% of the settled currents above**

The peaks are unaffected; the **settled currents in all four film runs carry that
offset**. Anything quantitative on steady-state current should be re-run on
`CR10_1mA`, or have the offset characterised (cell open at 0 V) and subtracted.

Caution for a fresh film: a healthier sample draws MORE than a degraded one, so
`CR10_1mA` has ~1.6× headroom rather than a generous margin. The overload warning now
fires within 100 ms, so the cost of being wrong is one aborted segment.

## 4. Every CV overloaded, and the CVs are probably fine

MEASURED: all four film runs flagged **`CURRENT OVERLOAD` on the CV segment only**
(t = 1.1–5.0 s), never on a chrono segment.

The CV runs the **procedure**, not `Ei` — `autolab_current_range` does not apply to it;
`FHGetSetValues` sets its own range and `FHPreCurrentRangingCV` auto-ranges. So the
CV's actual range is unknown and may be far more sensitive than 10 mA.

The recorded sweeps do **not** clip: `|I|max` 60–72 µA with exactly one sample at each
extreme, no rail.

**INFERRED:** overload ≠ clipping the recorded points. The instrument records staircase
*points*; the flag reflects the amplifier's *instantaneous* state, sampled at 10 Hz by
`pump()`. Each 10 mV step on a film drives a capacitive spike that decays before the
point is sampled, so a spike can overload momentarily while every recorded point is
fine — especially on a sensitive auto-selected range.

Two analyses were tried and **both were inconclusive**: per-window quantisation steps
(useless when current moves several quanta between samples) and a single-quantum fit
(residual ~0.5 for every candidate — `EI_0.CalcCurrent` is a calibrated value, not raw
counts). **We cannot say from this data whether the CV range-switched.**

The clean way to settle it is still the unwritten `probe_overload.py`: hold 0.1 V on
the 10 kΩ dummy at a deliberately too-sensitive range (`CR13_1uA` → 10 µA is 10× over),
and watch what the flag AND the recorded data do. That also answers whether the flag
latches or self-clears, which gates the `pump()` throttle in TODO.

## 5. `CR09_10mA` has a +1.6 µA zero offset — MEASURED

`20260911_test1`, 10 kΩ 1% dummy, three holds, fit `I = V/R + offset`:

```
R      = 9.977 kOhm     <- correct, so the slope is right
offset = +1.605 uA      <- additive, 0.016% of full scale
residuals: +0.0, -16.4, +0.0 nA   (half a quantum)
```

Nothing wrong with the driver, the dummy or the sampling — it is the range's own zero.
Unremarkable for a coarse range, and the reason the raw numbers read 8.6 and 9.2 kΩ.

## 6. Defects found and fixed today

1. **The pre-dedoping potential was labelled bare `"Potential (vs Vref):"`** while every
   other potential field names itself. −0.5 V intended for dedoping went in there, so
   `film1` and `film2` dedoped at 0 V for their whole ladders. Nothing malfunctioned —
   the settings, metadata and data were all faithful; the label was the defect. Now
   named, with a test that fails any potential field too generic to tell apart.
2. **The Results dropdown hid the tail of a long run.** A 14-segment ladder against Qt's
   default `maxVisibleItems` of 10 made `Doping/Dedoping 4` and `5` invisible — the run
   looked truncated when every file was on disk. Now 26, with a forced list-view popup
   (Qt ignores the setting under a native popup, which Windows uses).
3. **The overload warning gave CV-inappropriate advice** — it told a procedure-mode
   segment to raise `autolab_current_range`, which does nothing there. Now mode-specific.
4. **Connect buttons gave no feedback**, so a click that had landed looked ignored and
   got clicked again — two full connect cycles appear in the 12:0x crash log, on a USB
   stack that has failed three times under repeated open/close. Now disabled + repainted
   before the blocking call.
5. **`Ei` current range was bench.ini-only** — a per-sample value editable only by
   editing a file. Now a dropdown on the Parameters tab.
6. **Graph titles said `"Doping 4"`** and not what potential that was. Now
   `"Doping 4  (+0.600 V)"`, from a single shared definition of the ladder
   (`data.segment_potential`) that both potentiostat backends also use, so a title
   cannot drift from what was applied.

## 7. One GUI crash, before any run

12:0x, during/just after reference collection, no Python traceback — consistent with a
native/SDK failure rather than our code, and with this rig's history (2026-09-03 stale
link, 2026-09-09 WinUSB teardown). Cleared by a USB reset + Autolab power cycle. Nothing
was at risk; no run had started. Connect time is now logged so a creeping value can be
compared across days — whether a degrading link shows up that way is a SUSPICION, not an
established fact.
